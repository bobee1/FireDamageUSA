Short answer: To get smoke smell out of a house, remove the source, not just the smell. Air the house out, clean soot from every hard surface, replace HVAC filters, wash or dry-clean fabrics, and clean carpet and ducts. Odor that still comes back is trapped in walls, insulation or framing and needs professional deodorizing or sealing.

Smoke smell is one of the hardest parts of a fire to live with. It hangs in closets, comes back when the heat runs, and clings to everything you own. The good news is that it can almost always be removed. This guide covers the steps you can take yourself, and the point where professional smoke odor removal makes more sense. It applies to house fires, wildfire smoke, kitchen fires and furnace puff-backs.

Stay safe while you work. If the fire department has not cleared the house, don't go in. Wear gloves and a well-fitted N95 respirator around soot and ash.

Why does smoke smell linger in a house?

Smoke smell lingers because smoke is made of tiny particles and gases that settle into porous materials. Hot smoke rises and travels through every gap, so it reaches attics, wall cavities, ducts and closets far from the fire. Paint, drywall paper, unsealed wood, carpet, upholstery and insulation all absorb it.

Heat and humidity release the odor again. That is why a house can smell fine on a cool morning and smoky on a warm afternoon. Sprays, candles and plug-in scents only cover the smell for a short time. To remove it for good, you have to clean, remove or seal whatever is holding it.

How to get smoke smell out of a house, step by step

  1. Air the house out. Open windows and run box fans pointed outward to push smoky air out. Do this only when outdoor air is clean, not during a nearby wildfire.
  2. Remove the worst items. Take out burned debris, trash and food exposed to smoke or heat. Bag soft items you will wash later.
  3. Replace HVAC filters. Swap the furnace or air handler filter now, and again after cleaning. A dirty filter keeps blowing smoke odor through the house.
  4. Vacuum with HEPA. Vacuum floors, upholstery and drapes with a HEPA vacuum. The EPA recommends a HEPA-type vacuum for dusty surfaces after smoke events.
  5. Clean hard surfaces. Wash walls, ceilings, cabinets, floors and trim. Work in small sections and rinse as you go. Our guide on how to clean soot off walls explains the methods.
  6. Wash fabrics. Launder curtains, bedding and clothing, or send them to a dry cleaner who handles smoke. See how to get smoke smell out of clothes.
  7. Clean carpets and rugs. Hot water extraction removes residue from carpet fibers. If the smell stays, the carpet pad underneath may need replacing.
  8. Clean the ducts. If the HVAC ran during or after the fire, have the ducts, registers and air handler cleaned.
  9. Seal what still smells. Use a stain- and odor-blocking sealing primer on cleaned walls and ceilings before you repaint.

How to get rid of smoke smell in a house fast

If you need the house livable quickly, focus on the air and the biggest surfaces first. Fast results come from removing smoky air and capturing particles, then cleaning the rooms you use most.

  • Run HEPA air cleaners in the bedrooms and living area.
  • Set the HVAC to recirculate and use a filter rated MERV 13 if your system can handle it. The EPA suggests both during wildfire smoke.
  • Bag smoky fabrics and get them out of the house until they are washed.
  • Wipe hard surfaces in one room at a time, starting with the room you sleep in.
  • Set bowls of baking soda or activated charcoal in closed rooms to absorb some lingering odor.

These steps can make a home bearable within a day or two. They don't replace cleaning the ducts, carpet and walls.

Do ozone machines, foggers and hydroxyl generators work?

Professional deodorizing equipment works on odor that cleaning can't reach, but each type has limits. Thermal foggers push a deodorizer into cracks the way smoke traveled. Hydroxyl generators break down odor slowly and are generally used with people present. Ozone generators are strong but risky.

The EPA says ozone can damage the lungs and that no federal agency has approved ozone generators for use in occupied spaces. It also reports that ozone at safe levels is not effective at removing many odor-causing chemicals. If ozone is used after a fire, the house must be empty of people, pets and plants and aired out afterward. Our guide to hydroxyl generators, ozone and thermal fogging compares them in detail.

How to get fire smoke smell out of a house vs. cigarette smoke

Fire smoke and cigarette smoke need different approaches. Fire smoke leaves soot, ash and residue from burned plastics and building materials, often spread through the whole house in minutes. Cigarette smoke builds up slowly as a sticky yellow-brown film of tar and nicotine.

What to clean smoke-smelling walls with

For light smoke film, a mild soap or detergent and warm water work. The American Red Cross also lists a stronger mix: 4 to 6 tablespoons of trisodium phosphate (TSP) and 1 cup of household cleaner or chlorine bleach per gallon of warm water. TSP is caustic, so wear rubber gloves and rinse well. The Red Cross advises washing walls one small area at a time from the floor up, doing ceilings last, and not repainting until everything is fully dry.

Test any cleaner on a hidden spot first. Heavy or oily soot should be vacuumed and dry-sponged before any water touches it, or it will smear.

Wildfire smoke smell in a house

Wildfire smoke usually leaves a lighter film than a house fire, but ash can carry harmful residue from burned buildings. The EPA says ash from burned structures generally contains more harmful chemicals than forest ash, and advises against dry sweeping and leaf blowers. Mist ash before sweeping, then wet mop. Children and people with heart or lung disease should stay away from the cleanup.

How to prevent smoke smell from coming back

  • Replace HVAC filters again a week or two after cleaning.
  • Seal cleaned walls before painting instead of painting straight over them.
  • Keep windows closed and air on recirculate during wildfire smoke days.
  • Store a HEPA air cleaner where you can reach it quickly.
  • Don't put cleaned belongings back until rooms and ducts are done.

Local note: Wildfire smoke drives most odor calls in California and Oregon, while oil furnace puff-backs cause oily smoke smell across Maine and Connecticut. In apartment-heavy New York, smoke often reaches units far from the fire.

When should you call a smoke odor removal professional?

  • The smell is in more than one room after you have cleaned.
  • It gets stronger when the furnace or AC runs.
  • The fire department responded to the fire.
  • Insulation, carpet pad or framing may hold smoke.
  • You need written documentation for an insurance claim.
