Short answer: Does renters insurance cover fire damage? Yes. It pays for fire damage to your belongings, including smoke and soot damage, and usually pays for a hotel or temporary rental if your unit becomes unlivable. It does not cover the building itself; that's the landlord's policy. Renters insurance also includes liability coverage if you accidentally cause a fire that damages other units.

When an apartment or rental house catches fire, tenants often assume the landlord's insurance will take care of everything. It won't. The NAIC is blunt about it: your landlord's insurance will not cover your personal belongings. A renters policy, sometimes called an HO-4, is what pays to clean or replace your things and keeps a roof over your head during repairs. Much of that work is contents cleaning and pack-out, which saves belongings that look ruined but clean well.

This is general information, not insurance or legal advice. Policies and state laws differ. Read your policy and talk with your agent.

Does renters insurance cover fire damage?

Yes. Fire is a standard covered peril on renters policies. It doesn't matter whether the fire started in your unit, in a neighbor's unit, in a common area or outside the building. Accidental fires you cause are covered too, such as an unattended pan or a candle. Fires set on purpose are not.

Renters insurance typically has three main parts, and a fire can touch all of them.

CoverageWhat it pays after a fire
Personal property (Coverage C)Cleaning or replacing furniture, clothing, electronics and other belongings damaged by fire, smoke, soot or firefighting water
Loss of use / ALE (Coverage D)Extra costs to live elsewhere, such as a hotel, temporary rental and meals, while the unit is unlivable
Personal liabilityClaims against you if your accident damages the building or neighbors' property

The Maryland Insurance Administration lists the same core pieces: coverage for household contents and personal possessions, additional living expenses such as hotel stays and restaurant meals after a covered loss, and liability.

Does renters insurance cover smoke damage?

Yes, in most cases. Smoke damage from a sudden fire, including a fire in another unit or a nearby wildfire, is usually covered under personal property. That includes soot on furniture, smoke odor in clothing and bedding, and electronics damaged by soot.

Smoke from your own long-term smoking, or soot from candles built up over months, is typically treated as wear and tear and not covered. For the cleaning side, see how to get smoke smell out of clothes, and for coverage gray areas, what is considered smoke damage.

What the landlord's insurance covers vs. yours

After a rental fire, two or more policies usually respond. Knowing which one pays for what avoids delays.

ItemUsually paid by
Walls, roof, floors, built-in cabinets and fixturesLandlord's policy
Common areas: halls, stairs, laundry roomsLandlord's policy
Appliances owned by the landlordLandlord's policy
Your furniture, clothes, electronics and personal itemsYour renters policy
Your hotel and extra living costsYour renters policy
Improvements you paid for, such as shelving you installedOften your renters policy, up to a small limit
Damage you caused to the building or other unitsYour renters liability, if you're found responsible

The Maryland Insurance Administration notes that the landlord's insurance covers structural damage to the building but not your belongings or your liability to others.

Watch out: a damage waiver is not renters insurance

Some landlords charge a property damage liability waiver, sometimes shown on your rent bill. The Maryland Insurance Administration warns that a PDLW is not renters insurance and typically will not cover any of your belongings. If you only have a waiver, you likely have no coverage for your things after a fire.

Will renters insurance pay for a hotel after a fire?

Usually, yes. If the fire makes your rental unlivable, additional living expenses (ALE) pay the cost of living elsewhere above what you normally spend. The NAIC explains that ALE covers expenses above your normal living costs, such as a hotel or temporary rental, while the home is repaired.

ALE often has a limit, sometimes stated as a percentage of your personal property limit or as a time limit. Keep receipts for lodging, meals out, laundry, extra mileage and storage. Ask your insurer early what counts. The American Red Cross can help with emergency shelter for the first night or two.

How much will renters insurance pay?

Your payout depends on your personal property limit, your deductible, and whether the policy pays replacement cost or actual cash value. The NAIC notes that buying actual cash value coverage may save money at first but could cost you more if you need to replace damaged items, because it subtracts depreciation.

United Policyholders adds that depreciation is negotiable, and that replacement cost policies generally require you to replace items and submit receipts to collect the full amount. Jewelry, art, collectibles and firearms may have low limits unless they are scheduled separately, as the NAIC suggests checking with your agent.

How to file a renters insurance claim after a fire

  1. Get safe and get help. Stay out until the fire department says the building is safe. Contact the Red Cross if you need shelter.
  2. Call your insurer. Report the fire and get a claim number. Ask how ALE works and what receipts they need.
  3. Tell your landlord in writing. Ask about the building's timeline, access and your lease.
  4. Document your belongings. Photograph and video every room and closet before anything is cleaned or thrown away.
  5. Build an inventory. List items room by room with age and approximate cost. United Policyholders offers a free sample spreadsheet and suggests adding items as you remember them.
  6. Keep a claim diary. Note every call, the person's name and what was agreed, and confirm decisions in writing.
  7. Clean what can be saved. Ask your adjuster whether to use a professional contents cleaner. Many items that look ruined clean well.
  8. Read every check. United Policyholders warns that if a check says "final" and the amount isn't complete, ask for it to be reissued.

Renters insurance fire damage: what's often not covered

  • The building itself and the landlord's appliances.
  • Fires you set on purpose.
  • Gradual smoke or soot from smoking or candles.
  • High-value items above special limits unless scheduled.
  • Items belonging to roommates who aren't named on your policy.
  • Damage to your car, which falls under auto insurance.

Roommates, subletters and students

A renters policy usually covers only the people named on it and their relatives living with them. Roommates who aren't related should each have their own policy. Students living away from home may have some coverage under a parent's homeowners policy, often with lower limits. Check before you assume.

How to be ready before a rental fire

  • Buy a renters policy; the NAIC says the average one costs about $15 to $30 a month.
  • Choose replacement cost if you can afford it.
  • Take a video of each room and store it online.
  • Keep a fire extinguisher in the kitchen and test your smoke alarm.
  • Know two ways out of your building.

Local note: Apartment fires that spread smoke to many units are common in New York, Illinois and Washington, DC. Renters in Texas and Nevada often live in large complexes where one fire affects several buildings.
