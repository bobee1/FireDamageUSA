Short answer: A hydroxyl generator breaks down smoke odor slowly and is generally used with people present. An ozone generator works faster and stronger but is only safe in an empty, sealed space, because the EPA says ozone can damage the lungs. Thermal fogging pushes deodorizer into cracks and fabrics. None of them works unless the soot is cleaned first.

When smoke smell won't go away, people start looking at machines. Search results are full of hydroxyl generators, ozone machines and foggers for sale or rent. Each one can help, but each has a place, and buying the wrong one wastes money or puts your family at risk. Here is how they compare, and when professional smoke odor removal is the better route.

What is a hydroxyl generator?

A hydroxyl generator is a machine that uses ultraviolet (UV) light to make hydroxyl radicals from the water vapor and oxygen already in the air. Hydroxyl radicals are very reactive. They attack odor molecules and break them into simpler compounds, much as sunlight and outdoor air slowly freshen things outside.

The process is gentle and slow. A hydroxyl generator may run for several days to treat a house. Its big advantage is that it is generally used with people, pets and plants still at home, so you don't have to move out for treatment.

What is an ozone generator?

An ozone generator makes ozone gas, a form of oxygen with three atoms instead of two. Ozone reacts with odor molecules and breaks them down. At high levels it works faster than hydroxyl and reaches deep into porous materials.

The problem is that the same reaction harms people. The EPA says ozone can damage the lungs when inhaled, and that no federal agency has approved ozone generators for use in occupied spaces. The EPA also reports that ozone at levels within public health standards is not effective at removing many odor-causing chemicals. In other words, an ozone level safe to breathe is too weak to work, and a level strong enough to work is not safe to breathe.

After a fire, the EPA notes ozone may be used in unoccupied spaces as long as no people or pets are exposed. That is how professionals use it: empty house, sealed rooms, then a full airing out.

What is thermal fogging?

Thermal fogging heats a deodorizing solution into a very fine fog. The fog behaves like smoke did: it drifts into cracks, wall cavities, closets and fabrics and neutralizes odor where it settled. It is often used after cleaning to reach places a sponge can't.

The house must be empty during fogging. Crews cover smoke detectors and shut off pilot lights and other flames, and the space is aired out before anyone returns.

Why cleaning comes before any machine

Odor machines treat smell, not soot. If soot and smoke residue are still on walls, in ducts or in carpet, the residue keeps releasing odor, and the smell returns days or weeks later. The ANSI/IICRC S700 standard for fire and smoke restoration treats source removal and cleaning as the foundation of odor control.

The right order is:

  1. Remove burned debris and materials that can't be saved, such as smoky insulation and carpet pad.
  2. Clean every surface that smoke touched, including inside cabinets and closets.
  3. Clean the HVAC system and replace filters.
  4. Deodorize with fogging, hydroxyl or ozone, matched to whether the house can be emptied.
  5. Seal burned framing and stubborn surfaces with an odor-blocking primer.

Is a hydroxyl generator worth renting?

A rental hydroxyl generator can make sense for light, leftover odor after you have cleaned, especially if you can't leave the house. It is less useful for a whole-house fire smell, because it works slowly and can't reach odor inside walls or insulation. If you rent one, run it in closed rooms, one area at a time, and keep cleaning the source.

Can I use an ozone machine myself?

You can rent one, but use extra caution. Never run ozone with anyone home, including pets. Remove plants, and close doors to rooms you aren't treating. Afterward, air the house out fully before going back in. Ozone can also break down rubber, some plastics and fabric dyes with repeated use. If anyone in the home has asthma or lung disease, leave ozone to professionals or skip it.

Odor control during wildfire smoke

When wildfire smoke is outside, odor machines are the wrong tool. Focus on keeping smoke out. The EPA recommends closing windows and doors, setting the HVAC to recirculate or closing the outdoor air damper, and using a filter rated MERV 13 if your system can handle it. A HEPA air cleaner in one closed room gives you a clean-air space.

Signs a deodorizing job worked

  • No smoke smell when you come in from outside.
  • No smell when the heat or AC first turns on.
  • Closets and drawers smell neutral when opened.
  • Odor stays gone on warm, humid days.

Local note: Vacant homes after big fires, like those in Colorado and Hawaii, are where ozone treatment fits best. Families staying home through winter in Minnesota or Vermont usually do better with hydroxyl generators.

When should you call a smoke odor professional?

  • The odor followed a house fire, not just burnt food.

  • You can't safely leave the home for ozone treatment.
  • Insulation, carpet pad or ducts may hold smoke.
