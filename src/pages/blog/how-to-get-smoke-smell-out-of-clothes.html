Short answer: To get smoke smell out of clothes, shake off loose soot outside, then wash in cold water with your usual detergent plus an odor booster such as baking soda or a tablespoon of pure vanilla extract. Air-dry and repeat if the smell remains. Send dry-clean-only items to a cleaner who treats smoke. Don't use the dryer until the odor is gone.

After a fire, your closet can smell like a campfire even if flames never reached it. Smoke gets into fibers fast, and soot can stain if you treat it the wrong way. Most clothing can be saved with patience and the right washing method. For a whole house of belongings, professional contents cleaning and pack-out handles washing, dry cleaning and odor treatment in bulk, with an inventory for your insurer.

This guide covers washable clothing, dry-clean items, bedding, shoes and bags, plus what you should throw away.

Before you wash: protect your claim

If the smoke came from a fire you will claim on insurance, document first. Photograph clothing in the closet and on hangers, list what you have, and keep tags and receipts if you have them. The U.S. Fire Administration notes that items that did not burn may still be ruined by smoke or water, and your insurer will want to see them. Ask your adjuster whether to wash items yourself or send them to a cleaner.

How to get smoke smell out of clothes, step by step

  1. Take clothes outside. Shake each item gently to remove loose soot and ash. Don't brush soot in, which can push it deeper into fibers.
  2. Air them out. Hang clothes outdoors in fresh air for a few hours, out of direct sun if colors may fade.
  3. Read the care label. Separate machine-washable items from dry-clean-only, wool, silk and leather.
  4. Wash cold first. Hot water can set soot stains. Use cold water and your normal detergent.
  5. Add an odor booster. The American Red Cross suggests adding one tablespoon of pure vanilla extract to a cold wash. Baking soda or white vinegar in the rinse also helps with light odor.
  6. Air-dry and smell. Dryer heat can lock in odor. Let items air-dry, then check. Repeat the wash if needed.
  7. Use the dryer only when clean. Once there's no smoke smell, dry as usual.

Stronger washing for heavy smoke and soot

For clothing that can be bleached, the American Red Cross lists a stronger mix: 4 to 6 tablespoons of trisodium phosphate (TSP) and 1 cup of household cleaner or chlorine bleach in each gallon of warm water. The Red Cross also notes that products containing TSP can reduce odors in fabrics. TSP is caustic, so read the label and wear rubber gloves.

Test any treatment on a hidden seam first, as the Red Cross advises, and follow the maker's care instructions. Never mix bleach with vinegar or ammonia.

How to get smoke smell out of clothes without washing

Some items can't go in the washer. Try these first, then send them to a professional if the smell stays.

  • Fresh air: a day or two outdoors on a clothesline helps light odor.
  • Steam: a garment steamer can release odor from coats and drapes.
  • Sealed bag with baking soda: place the item in a large bag with an open box of baking soda for a few days.
  • Activated charcoal: works like baking soda in closets and storage bins.

These methods mask or absorb light odor. They won't remove soot.

What to throw away after smoke damage

Some things are not worth saving, or not safe to keep. The American Red Cross advises throwing away food that was exposed to heat, smoke or soot. The same goes for medicine and cosmetics. Beyond that, the decision is about whether an item can truly be cleaned.

United Policyholders notes that if a reputable cleaner says an item can't be cleaned, or it still smells after cleaning, it can be replaced and reimbursed under many policies. Photograph and list items before you throw them out.

Smoke smell in baby clothes and car seats

Take extra care with anything a baby or young child uses. Wash baby clothes and bedding separately with a fragrance-free detergent, and repeat until no odor remains. Car seats exposed to fire or heavy smoke should be checked against the maker's instructions; many makers advise replacing a seat after a fire. When in doubt, replace it.

How long does it take to get smoke smell out of clothes?

Light smoke odor often leaves in one or two washes. Heavy fire smoke may take three or more washes, or a professional cleaner. If an item still smells after several tries, the odor is likely deep in the fibers or filling, and replacing it may make more sense.

Keep clean clothes from picking the smell back up

  • Store clean clothes outside the house, or in sealed bins, until rooms are cleaned.
  • Clean closets and dressers before you put clothes back.
  • Replace HVAC filters so the system doesn't blow smoke odor into clean fabrics.
