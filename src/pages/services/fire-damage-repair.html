Quick answer: Fire damage repair rebuilds what the fire destroyed after cleanup is done: structural framing, roof decking and shingles, drywall, insulation, wiring, windows, cabinets, floors and paint. Repairs usually need building permits and inspections. In homes built before 1978, the EPA requires paid contractors who disturb painted surfaces to follow lead-safe work rules.

What fire damage repair includes

  • Structural assessment of framing, joists, rafters and the foundation.
  • Framing repair: replacing or reinforcing charred studs, joists and rafters.
  • Roof repair: new decking, underlayment and shingles where fire or vent cuts damaged it.
  • Electrical and HVAC work by licensed trades where your state requires it.
  • Insulation and drywall replaced, taped and finished.
  • Windows, doors and siding replacing board-up panels.
  • Cabinets, flooring, trim and paint to match the rest of the home.
  • Permits and inspections with the local building department.

The fire damage repair process

  1. Clean and dry first. Soot cleaning, odor removal and drying are finished before new materials go in, or new drywall picks up odor.
  2. Assess the structure. The crew checks how deep charring goes. Severe damage gets an engineer's review.
  3. Test older materials. The EPA recommends that suspect materials be sampled by an accredited asbestos inspector before renovation disturbs them.
  4. Write the scope and get approval. A line-item estimate goes to you and your adjuster.
  5. Pull permits. Structural, roofing, electrical and mechanical work is permitted with the local building department.
  6. Rebuild. Framing and roofing first, then rough-in trades, inspections, insulation, drywall and finishes.
  7. Final walk-through. You check every room against the scope before the job is closed.

Repair or rebuild?

Most fire-damaged homes are repaired, not torn down. A full rebuild is more likely when a large share of the framing burned, the roof collapsed, or the foundation cracked from heat. Your building official or a structural engineer makes that call. Insurance pays based on your policy's dwelling limit and whether it pays replacement cost or actual cash value.

Building codes, permits and licensed trades

Repairs must meet the codes your city or county enforces now, which may be stricter than when the house was built. That can mean new smoke alarms, wiring, egress windows or roof fastening. Most states and many cities require electrical, plumbing and HVAC work to be done by a licensed contractor, and many require a licensed general contractor for structural work. Ask who will hold the permit and check the license with your state.

Lead paint and asbestos in older homes

Lead-based paint was common in homes built before 1978, when it was banned for housing. The EPA's Renovation, Repair and Painting Rule requires anyone paid to disturb painted surfaces in those homes to follow lead-safe practices. Asbestos may be in old floor tile, ceiling texture, pipe wrap or siding. You cannot tell by looking, so testing before tear-out protects your family and the crew.

What affects the cost of fire damage repair

  • How much framing, roofing and wiring must be replaced.
  • Code upgrades required by your building department.
  • Finish level: cabinets, counters, flooring and fixtures.
  • Lead or asbestos testing and abatement.
  • Material prices and lead times in your area.

DIY or call a professional?

Painting a smoke-stained room after it has been cleaned and sealed can be a DIY job. Structural, roofing, electrical and gas work should not be. It needs permits and, in most places, licensed trades, and mistakes can void insurance or fail inspection.

Insurance notes

Ask your insurer whether your policy pays replacement cost or actual cash value, and whether it includes ordinance or law coverage for code upgrades. The NAIC lists additional living expenses among common coverages, which can pay for temporary housing during long repairs. Keep the repair estimate, change orders and receipts together. Before repairs, make sure fire damage cleanup and smoke odor removal are finished.
