Which fire damage restoration services do you need?

It depends on how big the fire was and how long ago it happened. Use this as a rough guide.

What happenedUsually needed
Small kitchen or grease fireSoot removal, smoke odor removal, cabinet and appliance cleaning
Furnace puff-back (oily soot from a misfiring oil burner)Soot removal through the whole house, duct cleaning, contents cleaning
Room fire put out by the fire departmentBoard-up, fire damage cleanup, smoke damage restoration, repairs
Fire next door or wildfire smoke nearbySmoke damage restoration, odor removal, air and duct cleaning

Fire damage restoration companies: what to ask

The U.S. Fire Administration warns that some companies offering board-up and cleaning after a fire are not honest, and suggests asking your insurer for names. Whoever you hire, ask these questions:

  • Will you give me a written scope and estimate before major work?
  • Do you follow the ANSI/IICRC S700 standard for fire and smoke work?
  • Will you test for lead and asbestos before tearing out materials in an older home?
  • Can you document the job with photos and itemized lists for my adjuster?
  • Who holds the building permit for repairs, and is the contractor licensed where my state requires it?
