Who writes our pages

Guides and service pages carry the byline {{editorial_byline}}. The editorial team picks topics from real questions people search for after a fire, researches each one, writes a draft, checks every claim against the listed sources and reviews the page before it is published. {{owner}}, owner of {{brand}}, is accountable for everything on the site.

We use software, including AI writing tools, to help with research and first drafts. A person checks each page against its sources before it goes live, and no tool decides which facts a page contains.

Sources we rely on

We favor primary and official sources, in this order:

  1. Federal agencies and national safety groups: the U.S. Fire Administration (USFA, part of FEMA), the Environmental Protection Agency (EPA), the Centers for Disease Control and Prevention (CDC), the Consumer Product Safety Commission, the National Fire Protection Association (NFPA) and the American Red Cross.
  2. State and local government: state insurance departments, state statutes, contractor licensing boards, fire marshals, and city or county building departments.
  3. Industry standards: published standards cited by name, such as ANSI/IICRC S700 for fire and smoke damage restoration and ANSI/IICRC S500 for water damage restoration.
  4. Company sources: used for a single figure only, and credited by name in the sentence.

Every source is opened and read before we cite it. A search result snippet never counts as a source.

How facts are checked

  • Each number, deadline and rule on a page links back to a source listed on that page.
  • Laws are cited with section numbers and effective dates, and confirmed to be in force on the day the page is published.
  • Health topics, such as smoke inhalation or soot exposure, follow public-health agency guidance only, and we tell readers to see a doctor about symptoms.
  • Insurance and legal topics carry a "general information, not advice" note and remind readers that policies differ.

Things we never do

  • Publish fake reviews, ratings, testimonials or before-and-after stories.
  • Invent statistics or prices. Cost content sticks to the factors that move a price.
  • Claim credentials, awards or partnerships we do not have.
  • Change what a guide says because a company paid us.
  • Pass off stock photos as pictures of our own jobs.

When pages are reviewed

Each service page and guide shows its last review date. Pages are reviewed at least every 12 months, and sooner when a law, code or safety guidance changes, or when a major wildfire season shows us something readers need.

How we earn money

{{brand}} is paid for restoration jobs that begin with a call or form request, which is why our phone number appears on every page. Payment does not change the facts in our guides. If a situation does not need a restoration crew, such as light smoke film from a single candle, our guides say so and explain how to handle it yourself.

Corrections

Found an error, an outdated rule or a broken link? Tell us through the contact page or by calling {{phone}}. Every report is reviewed. Material fixes are made promptly, and the page's review date is updated.
