General information only

The guides, checklists and answers on {{domain}} describe fire, smoke and soot damage, cleanup and insurance in general terms. They are not legal, insurance, medical, engineering or environmental advice, and reading them does not create a professional relationship. Building codes and laws vary by state, county and city, and no two insurance policies are the same.

  • Coverage questions: read your policy and ask your insurance agent or company.
  • Legal questions, such as a denied claim or a dispute with a landlord: ask a lawyer licensed in your state.
  • Health questions, such as coughing or headaches after smoke exposure: ask a doctor.
  • Structural questions, such as burned framing, a sagging roof or heat-damaged beams: ask a licensed contractor or structural engineer.

Safety first

Burned buildings can hide weak floors, falling debris, damaged wiring and leaking gas. Do not go inside until fire officials say it is safe, and do not restore power or gas yourself. Nothing on this site replaces instructions from your fire department, utility company or building inspector.

Arrival times

Our phone line is open 24/7. How fast a crew reaches a property depends on distance, weather, road access, whether the site has been released, and how many jobs are open at once, for example during a large wildfire. We do not promise a set arrival time.

Prices

We do not post prices on this website. Any cost factors we mention are general. A job's price is set in a written estimate or agreement after the property has been inspected.

Licensing, permits and testing

Rules for restoration, demolition, electrical and construction work differ between states and cities. Rebuilding after a fire often needs a building permit, and work that disturbs paint in homes built before 1978 falls under EPA lead-safe rules. Before repairs start, ask to see the licenses, permits and test results your state and city require.
