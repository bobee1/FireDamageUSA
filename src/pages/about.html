What SootMend does

Firefighters put the fire out. What they leave behind is a building that is open to the weather, soaked from hoses, and coated in soot that keeps damaging surfaces for days. {{brand}} handles that next part. One call to {{phone}} starts the work: board-up and tarping, water removal, soot and smoke cleaning, odor removal, contents cleaning and the repairs that follow.

We take calls {{availability}} from homeowners, renters, landlords, property managers and business owners anywhere in the United States. We have been in business for {{years}} years.

How a call to SootMend works

  1. You call. Tell us the address, what burned, whether the fire department has released the property, and whether anyone is living there now. If it is safe to take them, phone photos help.
  2. A crew assesses the damage. They check the structure, the power and the gas before work starts. Then they map where heat, smoke and water went, and write up the scope for you and your insurer.
  3. The property is secured. Broken windows and doors get boarded, and roof holes get tarped. Water left by fire hoses is pumped out so mold does not start.
  4. Soot and smoke are cleaned. The method depends on the residue. Dry sponges suit light, powdery soot, and wet cleaning suits oily soot. Air scrubbers with HEPA filters run during the work. Odor treatment goes deeper than air fresheners.
  5. Repairs and rebuild. Burned drywall, insulation, flooring and trim are replaced. Then paint, finishes and a final walk-through with you.

Why the first few days matter

Soot is acidic. Left on surfaces, it can etch glass, stain grout, yellow plastics and pit metal fixtures within days. Wet drywall and carpet from firefighting can start growing mold in 24 to 48 hours, the window the EPA gives for drying wet materials. Quick action keeps more of your home and belongings worth cleaning instead of replacing.

How we work

  • Safety before speed. Nobody enters until fire officials release the scene and power and gas are confirmed safe.
  • Written scope first. You see what will be cleaned, what will be replaced and why, before the work starts.
  • Industry methods. Crews follow the cleaning steps in the ANSI/IICRC S700 standard for fire and smoke damage restoration.
  • Testing in older homes. Homes built before 1978 may have lead paint, and older materials may hold asbestos. Suspect materials get tested before demolition, following EPA lead-safe and asbestos guidance.
  • Insurance-ready records. Photos, moisture readings and itemized lists that you and your adjuster can use.
