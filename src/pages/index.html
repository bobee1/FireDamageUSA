What a house fire leaves behind

Most of the damage after a fire is not where the flames were. Smoke travels through the whole house, and soot settles on walls, ceilings, ducts and belongings far from the burned room.

That is why a fire job is more than a repair job. Smoke damage restoration and soot removal often cover far more square feet than the burned area, and water from firefighting needs drying before mold starts.

The fire damage restoration process

Every job follows the same order, though small fires may skip steps. Crews work to the methods in the ANSI/IICRC S700 standard for fire and smoke damage restoration, first published in 2025.

  • Inspect and test each surface to choose dry or wet cleaning.
  • Secure openings with plywood and tarps.
  • Remove water and debris, then dry the structure.
  • Clean soot and smoke from structure, HVAC ducts and contents.
  • Deodorize what cleaning alone cannot fix.
  • Rebuild drywall, framing, roofing, floors and paint.

What affects the cost of fire damage restoration

No honest company can price a fire job before seeing it. These are the factors that move the number:

  • Size and spread: how many rooms the smoke reached, not only the burned area.
  • Type of soot: dry, powdery soot cleans faster than oily, wet or protein residue.
  • Water damage: how much water firefighters used and how long it sat.
  • Materials: what can be cleaned versus what must be torn out and replaced.
  • Contents: how many belongings need pack-out, cleaning and storage.
  • Testing and code: lead or asbestos testing in older homes, permits and code upgrades during rebuilding.

Signs you need professional fire damage cleanup

  • Black or brown film on walls or ceilings, even in rooms that did not burn.
  • A smoke smell that comes back after airing out the house.
  • Wet carpet, drywall or insulation from fire hoses.
  • Soot around vents or registers, which means it went through the HVAC system.
  • Yellowed plastics, pitted metal fixtures or etched glass.
  • Any burned framing, roofing or wiring.

After the fire: first steps

Stay out until fire officials say the building is safe, and leave the power and gas off. The U.S. Fire Administration says to call your insurance company right away and keep every receipt.
