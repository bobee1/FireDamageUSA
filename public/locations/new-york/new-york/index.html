New York has old housing: 38.2% of homes predate 1940. Fire can climb inside balloon-framed walls, and plaster holds smoke odor. Only 32.8% of homes are owner-occupied, so many fire calls come from renters, landlords and property managers.

Why fire damage in New York is different

Two in three New Yorkers rent, and most live in multi-unit buildings. A fire in one apartment sends smoke up stairwells, elevator shafts and halls to units many floors away, and water from fire hoses runs down to the floors below. Lithium-ion batteries in e-bikes and scooters have become a leading cause of serious fires in the city in recent years.

More than one in ten city homes still heat with fuel oil, often from a building boiler, so puff-backs can coat apartments in oily soot. Nearly four in ten homes were built before 1940. Brownstones, row houses and walk-ups often have old wiring and shared walls.

Home heating and fire risk in New York

Natural gas heats 64.4% of New York homes. Furnaces, boilers and water heaters need yearly service, and storage should stay clear of them. About 11.4% still burn fuel oil, which brings a risk of oily puff-back soot.

Tips for New York homeowners and renters

  • Charge e-bikes and scooters only with the maker's charger, away from exits, and never overnight.
  • Close the door behind you when you leave a burning apartment.
  • Keep hallways and fire escapes clear.
  • Ask your building about its boiler service schedule.

Insurance, permits and licensing in New York

Home insurance in New York is regulated by the New York Department of Financial Services, which takes complaints if a claim stalls. Co-op and condo owners need their own policy for interiors and belongings, because the building's policy rarely covers everything inside a unit. New York has no statewide general contractor license, so New York's building department sets permit and contractor rules for repairs. Fire damage questions in New York

Do you cover all of New York?

Yes. SootMend takes fire and smoke damage calls from across New York and New York City's five boroughs, including the Upper West Side, Harlem, Park Slope, Astoria and the Rockaways, and ZIP codes such as 10001, 10025, 10128, 11201.

How fast should cleanup start after a fire in New York?

Within a day or two. The EPA says to dry wet materials within 24 to 48 hours to prevent mold. Soot left on surfaces in New York homes keeps staining and eating into them.

Does insurance cover fire damage in New York?

Usually. Home and renters policies for New York homes cover sudden fire and smoke damage, wildfire included, up to your limits. Report the loss fast and ask about living expenses. For claim problems, contact the New York Department of Financial Services.

Who pays for smoke damage in my New York apartment from a fire on another floor?

The building's policy covers common areas and the structure. Your renters, co-op or condo policy covers your belongings and extra living costs. Report the damage to your landlord or board and your insurer.
