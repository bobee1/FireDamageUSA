Why fire damage in Mesa is different

More than three in four Mesa homes heat with electricity, and air conditioners run hard for months in extreme summer heat. Attic air handlers, overloaded circuits and pool equipment can overheat. About one in eight Mesa housing units is a mobile or manufactured home, and these burn fast and fill with smoke quickly.

On the city's eastern edge, homes border desert brush near the Usery Mountains and the Salt River. Maricopa County has had several FEMA fire management declarations since 2000. Stucco and tile resist flames, but smoke and fine soot still get in through vents.

Home heating and fire risk in Mesa

Electricity heats 76.5% of Mesa homes, so space heaters, cords and outlets drive most winter fire calls.

Fire damage questions in Mesa

Do you cover all of Mesa?

Yes. SootMend takes fire and smoke damage calls from across Mesa and Maricopa County, including Downtown Mesa, Dobson Ranch, Alta Mesa, Las Sendas and Red Mountain, and ZIP codes such as 85201, 85203, 85204, 85205.

How fast should cleanup start after a fire in Mesa?

Start as soon as the fire department lets you back in. The EPA says wet materials should dry within 24 to 48 hours to stop mold. Soot is acidic, so it keeps harming metal, glass and stone in a Mesa home.

Does insurance cover fire damage in Mesa?

Usually. Home and renters policies for Mesa homes cover sudden fire and smoke damage, wildfire included, up to your limits. Report the loss fast and ask about living expenses. For claim problems, contact the Arizona Department of Insurance and Financial Institutions.

Can a manufactured home in Mesa be repaired after a fire?

Sometimes. Light smoke damage can be cleaned. Fires that burn through floors, walls or the roof often total a manufactured home because the materials are thin. An adjuster and contractor can help you decide.
