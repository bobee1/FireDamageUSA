How to Clean Soot Off Walls and Ceilings Without Smearing It

By SootMend Editorial · Published November 19, 2026 · Updated November 19, 2026 · 5 min read

Short answer: To clean soot off walls, remove loose soot first with a HEPA vacuum, then wipe with a dry chemical sponge in one direction. Only after that, wash with a mild detergent or a TSP solution, working from the floor up and doing ceilings last. Never start with a wet rag: water smears soot into paint and makes stains permanent.

Soot on walls is one of the first things people try to clean after a fire, and one of the easiest to make worse. A quick swipe with a damp cloth leaves long black streaks that won't come out. The right order of steps keeps soot on the surface where it can be lifted off. This guide covers small areas you can handle yourself. For larger or oily soot, professional soot removal saves walls that DIY cleaning would ruin.

Before you start: safety and testing

  • Get clearance. Clean only after the fire department says the house is safe.
  • Protect yourself. Wear rubber gloves, goggles and an N95 or P100 respirator. The EPA says surgical masks and one-strap dust masks won't protect your lungs.
  • Turn off the HVAC so it doesn't spread soot while you work.
  • Cover floors and furniture with drop cloths.
  • Test a hidden spot with each method to see how the paint reacts.
  • Check the home's age. If it was built before 1978, avoid sanding or scraping; the EPA's lead-safe rules apply to paid work on painted surfaces in those homes.

What you need

  • HEPA vacuum with a soft brush attachment
  • Dry chemical sponges (also called soot sponges)
  • Mild dish detergent or degreaser
  • Trisodium phosphate (TSP) for heavy residue on washable walls
  • Two buckets, sponges and clean cloths
  • Stain- and odor-blocking primer for repainting

How to clean soot off walls, step by step

  1. Vacuum loose soot. Hold the brush just above the wall and let the vacuum pull soot off. Don't press or rub. The EPA recommends a HEPA-type vacuum for dusty surfaces after smoke.
  2. Dry-sponge the wall. Wipe the dry chemical sponge in long, straight, overlapping strokes in one direction. When the face turns black, cut or fold to a clean face. Don't wet the sponge.
  3. Check the result. On light, dry soot, the wall may be nearly clean now. Oily soot won't come off with a dry sponge; skip to a degreaser.
  4. Mix your wash. For light film, use mild detergent in warm water. For heavy residue, the American Red Cross lists 4 to 6 tablespoons of TSP and 1 cup of household cleaner or chlorine bleach per gallon of warm water.
  5. Wash from the floor up. The Red Cross advises washing one small area at a time, starting at the floor and working up, so drips don't streak soot below. Rinse each section right away with clear water.
  6. Do ceilings last. Wash ceilings after walls, as the Red Cross suggests.
  7. Dry completely. Let walls dry fully. The Red Cross says not to repaint until walls and ceilings are completely dry.
  8. Prime and paint. Use a stain- and odor-blocking primer, then your finish coat.

How to get candle soot off walls

Candle soot is usually dry and light, so it is a good DIY job. Vacuum gently, then dry-sponge the dark area in one direction. Follow with a mild detergent wipe if a shadow remains. To stop it coming back, trim wicks to about a quarter inch and keep candles away from drafts and walls.

Mistakes that make soot worse

  • Wiping with a wet rag first drives soot into the paint and spreads it.
  • Using a regular vacuum or shop vac blows fine particles back into the air.
  • Dry sweeping kicks soot and ash up. The EPA advises against it.
  • Scrubbing in circles grinds soot in. Use straight strokes.
  • Painting without primer lets stains and odor bleed through.
  • Running the HVAC before ducts are cleaned spreads soot to clean rooms.

After cleaning: stop the smell and the stains

Clean walls can still smell. Once washing is done, replace HVAC filters, wash fabrics and seal walls before repainting. The full plan is in how to get smoke smell out of a house.

Soot cleaning questions

What is the best cleaner for soot on walls?

Start with no liquid at all: a HEPA vacuum and a dry chemical sponge. Then use a mild detergent and warm water, or for heavy residue the Red Cross mix of trisodium phosphate (TSP) and bleach. Oily soot needs a degreaser.

How do you get soot off a ceiling?

Vacuum it with a soft brush attachment, then wipe with a dry chemical sponge in straight strokes. Wash ceilings last, after walls, as the Red Cross advises. Don't scrub textured or popcorn ceilings; older ones may contain asbestos.

Can Magic Eraser remove soot?

A melamine sponge can lift light soot from glossy paint, but it is mildly abrasive and can dull flat or eggshell paint. Use it only after vacuuming and dry sponging, and test a hidden spot first.

Will soot stains bleed through new paint?

Yes, if you paint straight over them. Clean the wall, let it dry completely, then use a stain- and odor-blocking primer before the finish coat.
