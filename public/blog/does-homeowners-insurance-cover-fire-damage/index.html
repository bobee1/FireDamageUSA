Does Homeowners Insurance Cover Fire Damage? What's Covered and What Isn't

By SootMend Editorial · Published November 19, 2026 · Updated November 19, 2026 · 6 min read

Short answer: Does homeowners insurance cover fire damage? Yes, in most cases. A standard policy pays to repair the house, replace or clean belongings, and cover extra living costs while you can't live at home, after your deductible and up to your limits. Smoke damage and wildfire are usually covered too. The main exclusions are fires you set on purpose and some vacancy situations.

Fire is one of the basic risks every standard homeowners policy is built to cover. Still, how much you get back depends on your limits, your deductible and whether your policy pays replacement cost or actual cash value. This guide explains what's covered, what isn't, and how a fire claim is paid, so you can plan the fire damage restoration of your home with fewer surprises.

This is general information, not insurance or legal advice. Every policy differs. Read yours and talk with your agent.

Does home insurance cover fire damage?

Yes. Fire is a covered peril on standard homeowners policies, including the common HO-3 form. It covers fires from cooking, heating, electrical problems, lightning, candles and most other accidents, even if you caused them by mistake. It also covers fire that spreads from a neighbor's home or from a wildfire.

What homeowners insurance pays for after a fire

The NAIC breaks a homeowners policy into several parts. After a fire, four of them matter most.

Coverage partWhat it pays after a fire
Dwelling (Coverage A)Repairing the house and attached structures: framing, roof, walls, wiring, plumbing, built-ins
Other structures (Coverage B)Detached garages, sheds and fences
Personal property (Coverage C)Cleaning or replacing furniture, clothing, electronics and other belongings
Loss of use (Coverage D)Additional living expenses such as a hotel, rent and meals while the home is repaired

Board-up, water removal, soot cleaning and odor removal are usually paid as part of the dwelling and contents claims, because they are needed to repair the damage and prevent more.

Is smoke damage covered by insurance?

Smoke damage is usually covered when it comes from a sudden fire. United Policyholders notes that smoke is typically a covered peril and that insurers usually pay to clean smoke and ash. The common dispute is whether an item should be cleaned or replaced. If a reputable cleaner says an item can't be cleaned, or it still smells after cleaning, it can often be replaced.

Smoke that builds up over time from candles, fireplaces or smoking is a different story. Many policies treat it as wear and tear. Our guide on what is considered smoke damage explains the difference.

Is wildfire damage covered?

Standard homeowners policies cover wildfire the same way they cover any fire. That includes burned homes and smoke and ash damage to homes that didn't burn. In high-risk areas, some insurers limit or decline coverage. Homeowners there may buy basic fire coverage from their state's FAIR Plan and pair it with another policy for other risks.

What fire damage is not covered?

Usually coveredOften not covered
Accidental kitchen, heating and electrical firesFires set on purpose by the policyholder
Fire from a neighbor's homeGradual soot from candles or fireplaces
Wildfire, including smoke and ashSome losses when the home was vacant for a long time
Lightning firesDamage above your policy limits
Water damage from firefightingBusiness property beyond small limits
Furnace puff-back soot (often named)Fires tied to illegal activity

Many policies exclude earthquake damage but still cover a fire that follows an earthquake. Check your exclusions and any endorsements.

Replacement cost vs. actual cash value

How you're paid depends on your valuation. The NAIC explains that you can insure on replacement cost, the cost to rebuild, or actual cash value, which reflects market value. United Policyholders adds that with replacement cost you generally must actually repair or replace items and submit receipts to collect the full amount, while an actual cash value check is usually the final payment.

How much will insurance pay after a fire?

Your payout is limited by four things: your coverage limits, your deductible, your valuation (replacement cost or actual cash value) and any special limits on items like jewelry or collectibles. The NAIC notes that you pay the deductible before the insurer pays. United Policyholders points out that your contents limit is often set as a percentage of your dwelling limit, and that high-value items may need separate coverage.

If your dwelling limit is too low for today's building costs, you may be underinsured. Extended replacement cost and ordinance or law coverage can help close the gap.

Additional living expenses after a fire

If the fire makes your home unlivable, loss of use coverage pays the extra cost of living elsewhere. The NAIC explains that many policies pay additional living expenses above your normal costs, such as a hotel or rental, while the home is repaired. Keep every receipt and ask early about time or dollar limits.

How to file a fire insurance claim

  1. Report the fire to your insurer right away and get a claim number.
  2. Get a full copy of your policy, not just the declarations page, and note deadlines.
  3. Protect the property with board-up and tarping, and keep the invoices.
  4. Document everything with photos and video before cleaning.
  5. Keep a claim diary of every call, name and decision, as United Policyholders recommends.
  6. Build your contents inventory room by room.
  7. Get a detailed repair scope and compare it with the adjuster's estimate.

How to protect yourself before a fire

  • Check that your dwelling limit matches today's rebuilding cost.
  • Ask about extended replacement cost and ordinance or law coverage.
  • Keep a home inventory with photos stored online.
  • Store a copy of your policy outside the house.

Local note: Where private insurers pull back, state plans fill in: the FAIR Plan in California and Texas, and Citizens in Florida and Louisiana. Each state page names its insurance regulator for complaints.

Homeowners insurance fire questions

Is smoke damage covered by homeowners insurance?

Usually, yes, when it comes from a sudden fire, including a neighbor's fire or a wildfire. Gradual smoke from candles, fireplaces or cigarettes is often excluded as wear and tear.

Does homeowners insurance cover a fire I caused by accident?

Generally yes. Accidents like a kitchen fire or a space heater fire are covered even if you caused them. Fires set on purpose by the policyholder are excluded.

Will my premium go up after a fire claim?

It may. Insurers can consider claims history at renewal, and rules on how much they can raise rates vary by state. Ask your agent, and compare the claim amount to your deductible before filing a small claim.

How long do I have to file a fire insurance claim?

Policies require prompt notice of a loss, and many set deadlines for proof of loss and for lawsuits. Deadlines vary by policy and state. Report the fire as soon as you can and read your policy's conditions section.

Does homeowners insurance cover fire damage to a detached garage?

Usually, under other structures coverage, which the NAIC describes as paying for structures not attached to the house, such as sheds and fences. Its limit is often a percentage of the dwelling limit.
