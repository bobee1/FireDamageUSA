Smoke Odor Removal

Sprays and candles cover smoke smell for an hour. Removing it means cleaning the source, treating what the smoke soaked into and sealing the rest.

  • Thermal fogging, hydroxyl, ozone

Quick answer: Smoke odor removal gets rid of the smell a fire leaves in walls, ceilings, ducts, carpet and belongings. It starts with cleaning the soot that holds the odor, then uses thermal fogging, hydroxyl generators or ozone to neutralize what remains, and seals surfaces that still smell. Masking sprays do not remove it.

What smoke odor removal includes

  • Odor mapping to find which rooms, materials and ducts hold the smell.
  • Source removal: cleaning soot and tearing out insulation, carpet pad or drywall that cannot be deodorized.
  • HEPA air scrubbing to capture odor-carrying particles in the air.
  • Duct cleaning and new HVAC filters.
  • Thermal fogging in empty spaces to reach cracks and wall cavities.
  • Hydroxyl or ozone treatment, matched to whether the space can be emptied.
  • Sealing of framing and drywall with an odor-blocking primer.

The smoke odor removal process

  1. Find the source. The crew checks each room, the attic, wall cavities and ducts, because odor hides where smoke traveled.
  2. Clean first. Soot and smoke residue are cleaned from all surfaces. Deodorizing over dirty surfaces does not last.
  3. Remove what cannot be saved. Smoky insulation, carpet pad and charred wood are removed.
  4. Clean the air path. Ducts, registers and the air handler are cleaned and filters replaced.
  5. Treat the odor. Thermal fogging, hydroxyl or ozone is run, often in rounds, with the space aired out between.
  6. Seal. Burned framing and stubborn surfaces are sealed with an odor-blocking primer before rebuilding.
  7. Check again. The house is closed up and warmed, then checked, because heat brings back odor that is still there.

The truth about ozone

Ozone is useful after a fire, but only with care. The EPA says that when inhaled, ozone can damage the lungs, and that no agency of the federal government has approved ozone generators for occupied spaces. It also reports that ozone at levels within public health standards is not effective at removing many odor-causing chemicals. That is why professional ozone treatment uses high levels in an empty, sealed building, with people, pets and plants out, and a full airing-out before anyone returns. Do not run a home ozone machine while you live in the house.

Why smoke smell lingers

Smoke particles are tiny and travel with hot air into every gap. They settle into porous materials: insulation, drywall paper, unsealed wood, carpet pad, upholstery and clothing. Each time the house warms up or gets humid, those materials release odor again. Air fresheners and open windows help for a few hours, but they do not reach the source. Removing the odor for good means removing or neutralizing what holds it.

Smoke odor in belongings

Clothing, bedding and curtains often come clean with proper laundering or dry cleaning; the American Red Cross notes that smoke odor and soot can sometimes be washed from clothing that can be bleached. Furniture, mattresses and electronics may need specialized cleaning or an ozone chamber off site. See contents cleaning and pack-out for how belongings are handled.

What affects the cost of smoke odor removal

  • How strong the odor is and how far it traveled.
  • How much insulation, carpet pad or drywall must be removed.
  • Whether the home can be emptied for fogging or ozone.
  • HVAC duct length and access.
  • How many treatment rounds the odor needs.

DIY or call a professional?

A faint smell from burnt food usually fades with cleaning, fresh filters and airing out. Call a professional if the smell is in more than one room, comes back when the heat runs, followed a fire the fire department responded to, or is in the HVAC system. Insurance notes

Odor is hard to show in photos, so note which rooms smell and tell your adjuster. Odor removal after a covered fire is usually part of the claim. Keep your restoration company's written scope, which lists the rooms and methods used.

Smoke odor removal questions

Why does the smoke smell come back after cleaning?

Because the odor is coming from something that was not cleaned or sealed: insulation, the inside of walls, HVAC ducts, carpet pad or porous wood. Heat and humidity release it again. The fix is finding and treating the source, not adding more fragrance.

Is ozone safe for smoke odor removal?

Only in an empty space with strict controls. The EPA says ozone can damage the lungs and that no federal agency has approved ozone generators for occupied spaces. Ozone treatment after a fire is done with people and pets out, then the space is aired out before anyone returns.

What is the difference between hydroxyl and ozone?

Both break down odor molecules. Hydroxyl generators work more slowly but are generally used with people present, while ozone is faster and stronger but needs an empty, sealed space. Many jobs use hydroxyl in occupied areas and ozone only where everyone can leave.

What is thermal fogging?

Thermal fogging heats a deodorizing solution into a fine fog that travels the way smoke did, into cracks, wall cavities and fabrics, and neutralizes odor where it settled. The space must be empty during fogging and aired out after.

How long does smoke odor removal take?

After cleaning is done, odor treatment usually takes one to four days. Heavy odor from a large fire can need several rounds plus sealing and replacement of insulation or carpet pad.
