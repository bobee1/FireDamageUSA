Contents Cleaning and Pack-Out After a Fire

Many of your belongings can be saved after a fire. A pack-out moves them out of the house, gets them cleaned and stored, and brings them back when the home is ready.

Quick answer: Contents cleaning restores belongings damaged by smoke, soot and water after a fire. In a pack-out, a crew inventories and photographs each item, packs it, cleans it with methods such as ultrasonic baths, dry cleaning and ozone rooms, stores it during repairs, then brings it back. Cleaning often costs less than replacing.

What contents cleaning and pack-out include

  • Room-by-room inventory with photos, notes and box labels tied to each room.
  • Sorting into clean, possibly clean, and not salvageable, shared with your adjuster.
  • Careful packing and transport to a cleaning and storage facility.
  • Cleaning matched to each material: hand cleaning, ultrasonic, laundering, dry cleaning, ozone room.
  • Electronics sent to specialists when soot or water got inside.
  • Document drying for wet papers and photos, where possible.
  • Secure storage until repairs are done.
  • Pack-back to the right rooms, checked against the inventory.

The pack-out process

  1. Walk-through. You and the crew go through each room. Point out heirlooms, papers and anything you need right away.
  2. Inventory. Each item or box is photographed, listed and labeled by room.
  3. Sort. Items are marked for cleaning or as not salvageable. Nothing is thrown away until it is documented for your claim.
  4. Pack and move. Belongings are wrapped, boxed and moved to a facility.
  5. Clean. Each item gets the method its material needs, then odor treatment.
  6. Store. Clean items are stored in sealed, labeled boxes.
  7. Pack-back. When the house is finished, everything returns to its room and is checked off the list.

Why pack out instead of cleaning in place?

Belongings left in a house under repair pick up drywall dust and new soot, and get moved from room to room. Packing out protects them, gives crews open rooms to clean and rebuild, and lets each item be cleaned with equipment that only works in a facility, such as ultrasonic tanks and ozone rooms. Small jobs, such as cleaning one room after a kitchen fire, can often be done on site.

Clothing, bedding and soft goods

Smoke odor clings to fabric, but many fabrics come clean. The American Red Cross notes that smoke odor and soot can sometimes be washed from clothing that can be bleached, and suggests testing garments before using any treatment. Professional cleaners add odor treatment and can handle dry-clean-only items, wool, leather and drapes.

Food, medicine and items to throw away

The American Red Cross advises discarding food exposed to heat, smoke or soot. The same goes for medicine and cosmetics. Do not throw anything away before it is photographed and listed, because your insurer may need proof of what was lost. The U.S. Fire Administration notes that items that did not burn may still be ruined by smoke or water.

What affects the cost of contents cleaning

  • How many rooms and boxes need to be packed.
  • The type of residue on belongings.
  • Specialty items: electronics, art, documents, rugs.
  • How long items stay in storage.
  • Whether cleaning happens on site or at a facility.

DIY or call a professional?

You can wash dishes and machine-washable clothing from a light smoke event yourself. Call a professional for whole-house smoke or soot, heavy odor, upholstered furniture, electronics, important papers, or when you need a detailed inventory for an insurance claim.

Contents and your insurance claim

Personal property coverage pays for belongings damaged in a covered fire. Keep the pack-out inventory, photos and cleaning invoices together. If you made a home inventory before the fire, share it with your adjuster. If you did not, list what you can by room. Renters have contents coverage only if they bought a renters policy.

Contents cleaning and pack-out questions

What is a pack-out after a fire?

A pack-out is when a restoration crew inventories, photographs, packs and moves your belongings out of the fire-damaged home. Items are cleaned at a facility or on site, stored safely during repairs, and brought back in a pack-back when the home is finished.

What belongings can be saved after a fire?

Many hard items clean well: dishes, glassware, metal, many hard plastics, finished wood furniture, and most washable clothing and linens. Items in the burned room, porous items soaked by firefighting water, and anything heat-warped are often not worth saving.

What should be thrown away after smoke damage?

Food, drinks, medicine and cosmetics exposed to heat, smoke or soot should be thrown away, even if they look fine. Mattresses and pillows with heavy smoke odor, and stuffed toys that cannot be laundered, are usually replaced. Photograph items before you throw them away.

Will insurance pay for contents cleaning?

The personal property part of most homeowners and renters policies covers belongings damaged by a covered fire, including cleaning when cleaning costs less than replacing. Keep the inventory list and photos, and ask your adjuster how they want contents handled.

How long are belongings kept in storage?

Usually until repairs are finished, which can be a few weeks after a small fire or several months after a large one. Items are stored in labeled boxes tied to the inventory so they come back to the right rooms.
