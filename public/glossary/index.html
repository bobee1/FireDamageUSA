Fire Damage Glossary

Restoration crews, adjusters and contractors use a lot of jargon after a fire. Here is what the most common terms mean, in plain words.

  • 55 terms
  • A–Z
  • Linked to full guides

Quick answer: This fire damage glossary defines 55 terms you may hear after a house fire, a puff-back or a wildfire, and during an insurance claim. Each definition links to the guide or service page that covers it in depth. Use the letters below to jump to a term.

ABCDEFHLMNOPRSTUW

A

ACV (actual cash value)
What an item was worth when it was damaged: the cost to replace it minus wear and age. Some policies pay ACV unless you have replacement cost cover. Homeowners insurance and fire damage
Additional living expenses (ALE)
The part of a home or renters policy that pays extra costs of living elsewhere, such as a hotel, rent and meals, while your home is repaired. Also called loss of use. Renters insurance and fire damage
Adjuster
The person who inspects the damage and decides what the insurance company will pay. A staff or independent adjuster works for the insurer; a public adjuster works for you. Dealing with an insurance adjuster after a fire
Air scrubber
A portable machine that pulls room air through a HEPA filter to catch soot, ash and dust during cleanup. Smoke damage restoration
ANSI/IICRC S700
The industry standard for fire and smoke damage restoration, first published in 2025. It covers inspection, cleaning and odor control, but not reconstruction. The fire damage restoration process
Asbestos
A mineral fiber once used in insulation, flooring, ceiling texture and siding. Fire and demolition can release fibers, so older materials should be tested before tear-out. Fire damage repair
Ash
The powdery leftover from burned material. Ash from burned buildings can hold metals and other harmful residue, so it needs careful cleanup. Fire damage cleanup

B

Board-up
Covering broken windows, doors and wall openings with plywood after a fire to keep out weather, animals and intruders. Board-up and roof tarping

C

Char
Wood or other material that has burned black on the surface. Deep char weakens framing and usually means the piece is replaced. Fire damage repair
Chimney fire
A fire inside a chimney flue, usually from built-up creosote. It can crack the flue and spread heat and smoke into walls and the attic. What is soot?
Contents
Your belongings, as opposed to the building itself. Insurance usually lists them as personal property or Coverage C. Contents cleaning and pack-out
Creosote
A tar-like residue that builds up inside chimneys and stovepipes when wood burns. It is very flammable and causes most chimney fires. What is soot?

D

Declarations page
The summary page of an insurance policy. It lists your coverage limits, deductibles and the policy period. Homeowners insurance and fire damage
Deductible
The amount you pay toward a claim before insurance pays the rest. Homeowners insurance and fire damage
Depreciation
The loss in an item's value from age and wear. Insurers subtract it to find actual cash value. Dealing with an insurance adjuster after a fire
Dry chemical sponge
A rubber sponge, also called a soot sponge, that lifts dry soot from walls and ceilings without water. How to clean soot off walls
Dry soot
Powdery soot from fast, hot fires that burn wood and paper. It is the easiest type to clean. Soot removal
Dwelling coverage
The part of a homeowners policy that pays to repair or rebuild the house and attached structures. Often called Coverage A. Homeowners insurance and fire damage

E

Encapsulation
Sealing a surface, such as burned framing, with a stain- and odor-blocking primer so leftover smell and stains stay locked in. Fire damage repair

F

FAIR Plan
A state-backed insurer of last resort that sells basic fire coverage when private insurers decline a home. Homeowners insurance and fire damage
Fire Management Assistance Grant (FMAG)
FEMA aid that helps states fight wildfires that threaten to become major disasters. Each state page lists these declarations. All locations
Fuel-oil soot
Black, oily soot from an oil furnace or boiler, often spread by a puff-back. It smears with water and needs solvent cleaning. Soot removal

H

HEPA
High-efficiency particulate air. HEPA filters catch very fine particles such as soot, and are used in vacuums and air scrubbers during cleanup. Soot removal
HO-3
The most common homeowners policy form. It covers the house against all risks not excluded, and belongings against named perils such as fire and smoke. Homeowners insurance and fire damage
HO-4
The standard renters insurance form. It covers a tenant's belongings, liability and extra living costs, but not the building. Renters insurance and fire damage
HO-6
The standard condo owner policy. It covers belongings and the inside of the unit that the association's master policy leaves out. Renters insurance and fire damage
Hydroxyl generator
A machine that uses UV light to make hydroxyl radicals, which break down odor molecules. It works slowly and is generally used with people present. Hydroxyl generator vs. ozone

L

Lead-safe work practices
EPA rules for contractors who disturb painted surfaces in homes built before 1978, to keep lead dust from spreading. Fire damage repair

M

Mitigation
The first stage of restoration: steps that stop damage from getting worse, such as board-up, water removal and drying. The fire damage restoration process

N

Negative air
Setting up fans and filters so a work area has lower air pressure than the rest of the building, which keeps soot and dust from drifting out. Commercial fire damage restoration

O

Ordinance or law coverage
Insurance that pays extra costs to bring a damaged home up to current building codes during repairs. Fire damage repair
Ozone generator
A machine that makes ozone gas to break down odors. The EPA warns ozone can damage the lungs, so it is used only in empty spaces. Hydroxyl generator vs. ozone

P

Pack-back
Returning cleaned belongings to the home after repairs, checked against the pack-out inventory. Contents cleaning and pack-out
Pack-out
Inventorying, packing and moving belongings out of a damaged home so they can be cleaned and stored during repairs. Contents cleaning and pack-out
PAHs
Polycyclic aromatic hydrocarbons: chemicals formed when things burn incompletely. They are found in soot and smoke. What is soot?
PM2.5
Fine particles 2.5 micrometers across or smaller, found in smoke. The EPA says they pose the greatest health risk among particles. What is soot?
Pre-testing
Testing a small hidden spot before cleaning a surface, to pick a method that removes soot without causing damage. How to clean soot off walls
Proof of loss
A signed, sworn statement of what was lost and its value, which some insurers require within a set time after a claim. Dealing with an insurance adjuster after a fire
Protein residue
A thin yellowish film left when food burns. It is hard to see but has a strong, lasting smell. Smoke damage restoration
Public adjuster
An adjuster you hire, for a percentage of the claim, to prepare and negotiate your claim with the insurer. Most states license them. Dealing with an insurance adjuster after a fire
Puff-back
A misfire in an oil furnace or boiler that blows oily soot through the ducts and into the house. Soot removal

R

Replacement cost
Insurance that pays what it costs to repair or replace damaged property with new, without subtracting depreciation. Often paid in two parts. Homeowners insurance and fire damage
Roof tarping
Covering roof holes and burned roofing with heavy tarps fastened to battens until permanent repairs. Board-up and roof tarping

S

Scope of work
The written, line-by-line list of repairs and cleaning a job needs. You and the adjuster use it to agree on what will be done. Fire damage restoration cost
Smoke damage
Staining, residue, odor and corrosion left by smoke on surfaces and belongings, even far from the flames. What is considered smoke damage?
Smoke odor
The lingering smell from smoke particles that settle into porous materials. It returns with heat and humidity until the source is cleaned or sealed. How to get smoke smell out of a house
Soot
Fine black or brown powder left when something burns without enough oxygen. It is mostly carbon, mixed with chemicals from what burned. What is soot?
Space heater
A portable heater. Space heaters placed too close to beds, curtains or furniture are a common cause of winter house fires. What to do after a house fire
Structure fire
A fire in or on a building, as opposed to a vehicle or wildland fire. Fire damage cleanup
Subrogation
When your insurer pays you and then seeks repayment from the person or company that caused the loss. Homeowners insurance and fire damage

T

Thermal fogging
Heating a deodorizer into a fine fog that travels like smoke into cracks and fabrics to neutralize odor. Done in empty spaces. Hydroxyl generator vs. ozone
Trisodium phosphate (TSP)
A strong cleaner used to wash soot and smoke from walls and some fabrics. It is caustic, so wear gloves. How to clean soot off walls

U

Ultrasonic cleaning
Cleaning hard items in a tank of liquid where sound waves shake residue loose, used for dishes, metal and small items. Contents cleaning and pack-out

W

Wet soot
Sticky, smeary soot from smoldering plastics, rubber and foam. It needs degreasers rather than dry sponges. Soot removal
Wildland-urban interface
Areas where homes are built next to or among wildland vegetation, where wildfires can reach neighborhoods. All locations
